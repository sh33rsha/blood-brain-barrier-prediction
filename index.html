1. What is Blood-Brain Barrier Penetration (BBBP)?

The Blood-Brain Barrier (BBB) is a highly selective semipermeable border of endothelial cells that protects the Central Nervous System (CNS) from blood-borne neurotoxins and pathogens while maintaining brain homeostasis.

In modern drug discovery, predicting BBBP is a $100M+ challenge:

  • CNS Drugs (e.g. Alzheimer's, depression, pain): Must cross the BBB efficiently to reach brain targets.
  • Non-CNS Drugs (e.g. cardiac, antihistamines): Must avoid crossing the BBB to prevent unintended central neurotoxicity or side effects.

2. Predictive Analytics & QSAR Methodology

This project implements a Quantitative Structure-Property Relationship (QSAR) machine learning pipeline using 2,050 curated drug candidates from MoleculeNet:

  • Feature Extraction: Converts raw SMILES strings into 2,048-bit Morgan Fingerprints (ECFP4) and Lipinski 2D descriptors ($MW$, $LogP$, $TPSA$, $HBD$, $HBA$).
  • Scaffold Splitting: Uses Bemis-Murcko molecular scaffolds to partition train/test sets, preventing data leakage across chemical structural analogs.
  • Ensemble Modeling: Evaluates XGBoost and Random Forest classifiers to predict binary penetration ($BBB^+$ vs $BBB^-$).

Bemis-Murcko Scaffold Splitting vs. Random Splitting Benchmark
Pharma Standard

Standard Random Splits artificially inflate accuracy because structurally similar drug analogs end up in both training and test sets (data leakage). Bemis-Murcko Scaffold Splitting partitions compounds based strictly on their core chemical ring scaffolds, ensuring zero overlap between training and testing sets to simulate real-world drug candidate generalization.
